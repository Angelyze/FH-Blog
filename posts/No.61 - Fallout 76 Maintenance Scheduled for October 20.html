Bethesda confirmed Fallout 76 will go offline across all platforms on October 20 to deploy a fresh update.

If you were planning to kick back in Appalachia early Tuesday morning, you will need to adjust your schedule a bit. Bethesda announced via Steam and their official social channels that Fallout 76 is scheduled for downtime on Tuesday, October 20. The servers will go offline across every supported platform beginning at 10:00 AM ET / 9:00 AM CT to allow the development team to deploy an update. Whether you are running dailies on PC, working through public events on PlayStation, or managing vendor caps on Xbox, everyone is taking a collective break while the maintenance window is active.

Key facts

  • Confirmed via official Bethesda communications; coverage from Steam — New Vegas.
  • Maintenance begins Tuesday, October 20 at 10:00 AM ET / 9:00 AM CT.
  • Downtime affects players across all platforms: PC, PlayStation, and Xbox.
  • Servers will go completely offline while Bethesda deploys a new update.
  • Live server status updates will be tracked through the official Bethesda Status Portal.

What to Expect on October 20

Bethesda confirmed that the server outage hits every platform at the exact same moment. When 10:00 AM ET (9:00 AM CT) rolls around on Tuesday, October 20, active worlds will start clearing out, and you will see the standard disconnection notice pop up on screen. Once the shutdown begins, the login servers are locked down, meaning you will not be able to reconnect until Bethesda finishes deploying the update on the back end. Bethesda has not provided an estimated end time for the maintenance window in their initial alert. That is pretty standard procedure for these patches. Maintenance length typically hinges on the size of the patch client side, the volume of database maintenance required server side, and whether any unexpected issues crop up during deployment. Some minor maintenance rounds wrap up in an hour or two, while larger update rollouts can keep Appalachia offline for a longer stretch of the afternoon.

Where to Track Server Status

Whenever servers drop, community feeds inevitably flood with players asking if the game is down for everyone or if their local connection is acting up. To skip the guesswork, Bethesda directs everyone directly to the official Bethesda Status Portal (status.bethesda.net). The status portal is maintained by Bethesda's operations team and tracks the operational state of individual titles across each ecosystem. When servers begin coming back online or if maintenance requires an extension, the portal updates there before announcements propagate across wider social channels. If you are watching for the exact moment the green light is given, refreshing the portal or checking Bethesda's official Fallout channels is your most dependable route.

Preparing Your In-Game Routine

Because downtime cuts off access abruptly at 10:00 AM ET, it pays to manage your in-game routine before the clock runs out. If you log in early on Tuesday morning to knock out daily score challenges, complete daily ops, or reset vendor cap limits, aim to finish those tasks well ahead of the cut-off mark. There is nothing more frustrating than getting kicked halfway through an intensive event like Radiation Rumble or losing a workshop you just spent resources fortifying. Giving yourself a safe buffer of ten or fifteen minutes before the maintenance window prevents lost loot drops or unrecorded milestone progress. Once servers drop, keep your platform client running—whether you are using Steam, the Xbox app, or your console dashboard—so any client patch begins downloading as soon as Bethesda pushes the files live.

Patch Notes and Details Still Pending

The announcement published by Bethesda confirms that the downtime is strictly to deploy an update, but the initial post did not include detailed patch notes or a breakdown of specific bug fixes. Bethesda typically holds the full changelog until maintenance is actively underway or just concluding, at which point the complete notes are published across their official forums and update logs. Until those notes surface, we will not know whether this rollout is a targeted hotfix addressing recent community-reported bugs or a broader maintenance patch laying groundwork for upcoming content.
