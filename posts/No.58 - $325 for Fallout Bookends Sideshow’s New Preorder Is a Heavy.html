Sideshow is preordering new Fallout bookends for $325. Are these hand-painted polyresin pieces worth more than a Pip-Boy? Here is what we know so far.

Editorial note: This article is based on press reporting and has not been confirmed by the developer or publisher.

A new report from IGN highlights a premium set of hand-painted bookends that might just be the priciest way to organize your Vault-Tec guides.

I’ve seen a lot of Fallout merch over the years, from novelty bottle caps and replica Nuka-Cola bottles to wearable power armor helmets, but this latest find from Sideshow is definitely on the high end of shelf decor. According to a report by IGN, an officially licensed set of bookends has hit the preorder stage with a price tag that’s raising plenty of eyebrows in the community. While the retail listing is live on Sideshow's site, we're still waiting for Bethesda or official franchise channels to spotlight the piece directly.

Key facts

  • Reported by IGN; not yet confirmed by the developer or publisher.
  • Reported by IGN; not yet formally spotlighted on official Bethesda social channels.
  • The bookends are priced at $325 and currently available for preorder through Sideshow.
  • Each piece weighs four pounds and is sculpted from polyresin with hand-painted detailing.
  • Estimated shipping window is slated for November 2026 through January 2027.
  • The pair features hand-painted antique metal effects designed to match the retro-futuristic Fallout aesthetic.

The Price of the Apocalypse

Let’s address the elephant in the vault: that steep $325 price tag. We all expect a baseline markup when it comes to officially licensed gaming gear, but this leap puts the piece in rare territory for functional desk accessories. As IGN’s Jacob Kienlen pointed out, you can currently pick up a semi-functional Pip-Boy replica from the IGN Store for $300 and still have change left over. Even Dark Horse, which isn't known for bargain basement pricing, recently put out its own set of Fallout bookends for $179.99. Spending nearly double that amount just to keep your game guides and art compendiums upright feels like a luxury reserved for dwellers sitting on an endless stash of caps.

Heavy-Duty Craftsmanship

So what actually justifies that invoice? For starters, these aren't lightweight hollow plastic desk novelties. The listing notes that each bookend weighs four pounds on its own, meaning the combined pair packs eight pounds of heft designed to anchor heavy hardcovers without slipping across polished wood. Sideshow notes that the pieces are sculpted from polyresin and individually hand-painted to mimic the weathered, antique metal finish the Fallout series is famous for. Between the Bethesda licensing costs and the hand-finished treatment, the price structure mirrors limited-run resin statues rather than ordinary home office supplies, even if the sticker shock remains very real.

A Long Wait for Shipping

If you do decide to drop the caps, you need to be prepared for a substantial wait. The estimated shipping window currently runs from November 2026 through January 2027. That is an enormous lead time for any preorder, putting actual delivery roughly two years out from the initial order button. If there's any reassurance for hesitant buyers, it's that Sideshow’s standard policy includes a 30-day return window once the package finally arrives at your door. That at least gives you a safety cushion to inspect the paint job and resin quality in person before locking in your purchase permanently.

Who Is the Target Market?

As someone who spends way too much time tracking Fallout collectibles, I have to wonder where the line sits for most fans. For the average player, $325 easily covers a brand-new console, a stack of recent releases, or a handful of high-detail action figures. But for the dedicated statue crowd who already fill display cases with Sideshow’s premium format pieces, this is familiar pricing territory. It’s a very specific item tailored for high-end collectors who value physical weight and hand-painted sculpts over interactive gimmicks like wearable Pip-Boy screens.

Why fans are talking about this: At $325, these bookends are less about simply propping up books and more about serving as heavy, hand-painted statement pieces for dedicated statue collectors.

We are still waiting on Bethesda to formally highlight this Sideshow collaboration across their main promotional feeds. Until then, we are looking at this through the lens of retail listings and reporting from outlets like IGN. Dropping over three hundred dollars on bookends is a major commitment no matter how good your shelving looks, so make sure your Barter skill is maxed out before placing a deposit on that 2026 delivery date.

If you had $325 to spend on one Fallout display piece, would you pick up these heavy bookends or put the caps toward a wearable Pip-Boy replica?
