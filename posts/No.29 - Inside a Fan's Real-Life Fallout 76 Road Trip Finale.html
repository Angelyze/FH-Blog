Read about a fan's real-life West Virginia road trip, hitting iconic Fallout 76 spots like the Mothman Museum, Trans-Allegheny Asylum, and Helvetia.

Reddit user DaeganBro wrapped up an epic West Virginia pilgrimage, finding real-world lore and even a hidden Sunset Sarsaparilla cap.

There is something incredibly cool about seeing the places we spend hundreds of hours exploring virtually in the physical world. Reddit user DaeganBro recently wrapped up a personal road trip through West Virginia's most famous Fallout 76 landmarks, and their final leg is the exact kind of travel diary that makes me want to pack a bag immediately. After getting home late and effectively passing out from the sheer exhaustion of the road, they shared the highlights of their trek through the real Appalachia. From hunting down cryptid merch to exploring massive historical halls, it is a great reminder of how much real history Bethesda packed into the map we call home.

Key facts

  • Reddit user /u/DaeganBro shared the final leg of their real-life Fallout 76-themed road trip through West Virginia.
  • The trip included stops at Point Pleasant's Mothman Museum, the Trans-Allegheny Lunatic Asylum, and the historic town of Helvetia.
  • During an empty 90-minute tour of the asylum, the traveler discovered a handmade Sunset Sarsaparilla bottle cap left by a previous Fallout fan.
  • The traveler plans to follow this up with a Fallout: New Vegas road trip in Arizona to bond with their brother, a cancer survivor.
  • The asylum tour was conducted by a guide named Bryce and covered all four floors plus the Civil War Wing.

Merch and Mystery at the Mothman Museum

The final stretch of the journey kicked off in Point Pleasant at the Mothman Museum. For any Fallout 76 player, this is basically holy ground. DaeganBro reported that the staff was incredibly welcoming, which always makes these trips better when the locals are in on the fun. They walked away with some great merch to commemorate the stop—because you can’t exactly visit the home of the Mothman and not bring back a souvenir for your real-life C.A.M.P. It is always fun to see these local spots embrace the weird, wonderful cryptid culture that Bethesda leaned so heavily into for the game's atmosphere.

A Quiet Tour of the Trans-Allegheny Lunatic Asylum

Next on the itinerary was the Trans-Allegheny Lunatic Asylum—known to players as Fort Defiance, the imposing headquarters of the Appalachian Brotherhood of Steel. This wasn't just a quick photo op; DaeganBro and their mother ended up getting a completely private 90-minute historical tour of all four floors and the Civil War Wing. Led by their guide, Bryce, the quiet tour was a massive win. DaeganBro mentioned that having the place to themselves was a relief, as it helped them avoid the sensory overload that often comes with crowded tourist destinations. They spent the time learning about the complex and often grim evolution of mental health treatments, which adds a heavy layer of context to all those scorched-filled halls we clear out in-game.

A Hidden Sunset Sarsaparilla Easter Egg

While exploring the asylum's museums before the official tour started, DaeganBro spotted a brilliant little detail left behind by another wanderer: a handmade Sunset Sarsaparilla bottle cap. It is a tiny, unsanctioned crossover that perfectly highlights how the Fallout community interacts in the wild. Finding a Mojave relic inside an Appalachian landmark is the exact kind of easter egg that makes these real-world trips so rewarding. It’s a reminder that no matter where you go in the states, there’s probably another fan who has been there, leaving a little piece of the wasteland behind for the next person to find.

Missing the Masks in Helvetia

The road trip wrapped up in Helvetia, the quiet mountain town famous for the annual Fasnacht Festival. While DaeganBro got to see the beautiful historic buildings that look like they were pulled straight from the game engine, they hit a classic road-trip snag: the Fasnacht mask museum was closed. They took full ownership of the oversight, admitting they forgot to check the operating hours beforehand. We've all been there, arriving at a destination only to find the door locked. Still, even without the masks, walking the streets of real-life Helvetia is a bucket-list item for anyone who has spent hours marching alongside those slow-moving Protectrons during the seasonal event.

Why fans are talking about this: Real-life Fallout tourism isn't just about matching game screenshots to real buildings—it's about the unexpected connections you make with history and other fans along the way.

It is always a treat to see folks actually get out there and explore the real-world spaces that inspired our favorite digital wastelands. Huge thanks to DaeganBro for sharing their journey with the community, and a massive shout-out to their older brother for beating cancer—that is the best news I've heard all week.
