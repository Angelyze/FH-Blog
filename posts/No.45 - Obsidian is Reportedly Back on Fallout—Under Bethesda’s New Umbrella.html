A new GameSpot report suggests Obsidian is developing a new Fallout project while moving under the Bethesda Softworks umbrella. Here is what we know.

Editorial note: This article is based on press reporting and has not been confirmed by the developer or publisher.

A major Xbox restructuring reportedly puts the New Vegas creators back in the driver's seat for a new Fallout title.

We’ve been waiting years for the words 'Obsidian' and 'Fallout' to end up in the same sentence again, and it looks like it’s finally happening—though maybe not how we expected. According to a new report from GameSpot detailing internal shifts at Microsoft, Xbox is restructuring its RPG studios, placing Obsidian Entertainment directly under the Bethesda Softworks business unit to collaborate on a new entry in the post-apocalyptic franchise. While we haven't seen a splashy public reveal trailer yet, the internal memos surfacing around Xbox's strategic reset are pointing toward a massive shift in how Fallout games get built.

Key facts

  • Reported by GameSpot; not yet confirmed by the developer or publisher.
  • Obsidian Entertainment will reportedly operate under the Bethesda Softworks business unit.
  • Xbox CCO Matt Booty reportedly confirmed a new Fallout project is in development at Obsidian.
  • The project is a collaboration between Obsidian and Bethesda Game Studios.
  • This follows significant layoffs at Obsidian and the reported cancellation of an Avowed sequel.

The New Corporate Map

The organizational landscape at Xbox is shifting again, and this time it hits right at home for Fallout fans. According to the report, Xbox chief content officer Matt Booty outlined the new structure on Xbox Wire, stating that Bethesda will expand its remit to include Obsidian. The studio will continue handling current projects like Grounded while taking on a new Fallout project in direct collaboration with Bethesda Game Studios. This doesn't mean Bethesda Game Studios is physically absorbing Obsidian into one office; rather, it mirrors the operational setup parent company ZeniMax Media maintains with id Software. Obsidian retains its studio identity and creative culture, but will now report through the Bethesda business unit—theoretically smoothing out licensing, lore alignment, and cross-studio tech pipelines.

A New Fallout in the Works

The biggest takeaway here is the explicit mention of a 'new Fallout project' actively entering development at the studio that delivered New Vegas back in 2010. Booty's remarks indicate that Obsidian won't be abandoning ongoing support for its live hits, but will instead co-develop this new wasteland entry alongside Bethesda Game Studios. We still don't know whether this project represents an early runway toward Fallout 5 or an ambitious spin-off designed to bridge the massive gap while BGS pours its primary development bandwidth into The Elder Scrolls VI. Regardless of the exact numbering, the report suggests there are now at least three distinct Fallout 'pies in the oven' across Xbox's publishing slate—a tempo the franchise hasn't enjoyed in over a decade.

A Heavy Price for the Pivot

This news arrives against a distinctly grim backdrop across Microsoft's gaming division. The report highlights that Obsidian lost roughly 25% of its workforce during Xbox's initial major reset in July, part of a wider wave that has cut hundreds of staff across multiple teams. That internal shake-up reportedly claimed an unannounced Avowed sequel, forcing the veteran RPG house—known for classics from Knights of the Old Republic II and Pillars of Eternity to The Outer Worlds—to pivot its core creative staff directly toward Fallout. It’s an undeniably bittersweet pill to swallow. We may finally be getting the developer pairing players have begged for since Microsoft purchased both parent companies, but it's arriving on the heels of canceled passion projects and deep studio cuts.

The 'id Software' Model

By folding Obsidian's reporting structure under the Bethesda umbrella, Xbox appears to be trying to replicate the durable ZeniMax/id Software production model. Under that arrangement, specialized studios share engine tech, internal tools, and high-level production oversight while fiercely protecting their own design sensibilities. For Fallout, this could give Obsidian engineers direct access to Bethesda's internal engine revisions and immediate contact with Todd Howard’s lore leads without the layers of corporate bureaucracy that separated them during the New Vegas era. If Xbox wants to keep franchise momentum rolling instead of waiting eight years between releases, getting its two veteran RPG teams on the same internal Slack channel is the obvious move.

Why fans are talking about this: Obsidian and Bethesda working together on Fallout is the pairing fans have spent over a decade asking for, even if the corporate shakeup behind it is messy.

While Booty's reported memo lays out clear operational plans, we are still waiting for a proper, public-facing announcement with logos and concept art from Bethesda or Obsidian to establish the project's exact scope and timeline. Until we see a title card, keep your expectations grounded. Even so, knowing Obsidian's writers and designers are officially back on the franchise ledger makes the long wait for the next wasteland trek a lot easier to stomach. We'll be keeping a close eye on both studios' channels as development gets moving.
