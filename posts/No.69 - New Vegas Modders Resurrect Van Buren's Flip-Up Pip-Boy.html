New Vegas modders have resurrected Van Buren's flip-up Lil Pip 3000 wrist computer alongside classic Wattz energy weapons in a new standalone release.

Editorial note: This article is based on press reporting and has not been confirmed by the developer or publisher.

Rock Paper Shotgun reports on the Lil Pip 3000 standalone release from the team rebuilding Black Isle's cancelled Fallout 3.

Every year around Bombs Drop Day, the modding scene drops standalone slices of projects that take years to piece together. According to recent reporting from Rock Paper Shotgun, the team behind the ambitious Fallout: The New West project has just released the Lil Pip 3000 for Fallout: New Vegas, pulling directly from Black Isle's cancelled Van Buren design documents. As with any community remake or press report, Bethesda and Obsidian haven't commented on the project, but for players living in the Mojave, it's an absurdly fun piece of rescued history that finally brings a piece of the 'lost' Fallout 3 into our hands.

Key facts

  • Reported by Rock Paper Shotgun; not yet confirmed by the developer or publisher.
  • Created by the Fallout: The New West team based on design docs from Black Isle's cancelled Van Buren project.
  • The Lil Pip 3000 features custom animations where the display lid actually flips open when used.
  • Lore places the device at Tibbets Prison before an unnamed ranger deposited it near Nipton.
  • Accompanied by a five-gun Wattz Weapon Pack with Tale of Two Wastelands integration.

The Canned History of the Lil Pip

The report from Rock Paper Shotgun highlights that this quirky wrist computer isn't an original design cooked up out of nowhere. It comes straight from the design documents of Black Isle Studios' original, unreleased Fallout 3—codenamed Van Buren. In that canned game's lore, the Lil Pip was standard issue for inmates at Tibbets Prison, an automated facility managed by an AI overlord that the New West team has already recreated for their own project demo. For this standalone New Vegas mod, the creators bridged the lore gap by placing it in the Mojave, left behind near Nipton by an unnamed ranger.

Why That Flip Animation Actually Matters

The modders made their central feature plain in all caps: IT FLIPS UP. Rather than staying as a rigid, blocky forearm console like the Pip-Boy 3000 you get from Doc Mitchell, the Lil Pip uses custom animations so the display lid pops up whenever your character raises their arm. Rock Paper Shotgun noted it carries a bit of a rugged children's toy aesthetic, but it still fits the grounded retro-futurism of the classic era without looking out of place during a firefight. Functionally, it handles standard Pip-Boy interfaces and integrates with existing UI companion mods, making it a surprisingly viable replacement for your standard wrist-mounted hardware.

Packing Classic Wattz Firepower

Alongside the wrist unit, the team behind Fallout: The New West also shipped a Wattz Weapon Pack. The report details five classic-style energy weapons included in the bundle, featuring a newly assembled burst pistol derived from Van Buren assets alongside custom firing animations. If you happen to run Tale of Two Wastelands to stitch Fallout 3 and New Vegas together, the mod also distributes these Wattz weapons across the Capital Wasteland loot pool, ensuring your energy-weapon build feels a bit more like a relic from the Black Isle days.

Check Your Load Order Before Heading In

If you plan on installing either download, this isn't a simple drag-and-drop affair. According to the release details, both mods require modern scripting backbones: NVSE, JIP LN NVSE, JohnnyGuitar NVSE, and the kNVSE Animation Plugin. The weapon pack demands even more housekeeping, calling for the Gun Runners' Arsenal DLC, ShowOff xNVSE, ISControl Enabler, and Sound Extender to function correctly without glitching out your animations. It's a heavy list of requirements, but for those of us who have spent years tracking the development of the New West project, it's a small price to pay for such a high-fidelity piece of fan-made history.

Why fans are talking about this: Pulling forgotten Black Isle design specs into a playable, animated New Vegas mod is the exact brand of obsessive preservation that keeps this scene alive.

While standalone mod projects like Fallout: The New West remain fan-driven efforts without official publisher involvement, they offer a tangible glimpse into Black Isle design concepts that were nearly lost to time. Just ensure your script extensions are current before venturing south of Nipton to grab it. It's a great reminder that even when a game is cancelled, the ideas behind it have a way of surfacing in the Mojave eventually.
