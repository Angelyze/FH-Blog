ZeniMax Media has issued a swift cease-and-desist to shut down 'Fallout: New York,' an AI-generated browser game that real fans labeled post-war slop.

ZeniMax Media officially shut down the controversial AI-generated project after just three days online, enforcing its trademark rights.

We have some wild news from the community this week, and it is a sharp reminder of why copyright laws exist. As first reported by Insider Gaming, ZeniMax Media has officially issued a cease-and-desist order to shut down 'Fallout: New York,' a browser-based project built entirely with generative AI. The project, which lasted a mere three days online, was quickly targeted by ZeniMax's legal team to protect the franchise's trademark and reputation.

Key facts

  • Confirmed via official Bethesda communications; coverage from Insider Gaming.
  • A user named CHRIS FIRST launched 'Fallout: New York' on September 28, built entirely using the Claude Opus 5.5 AI model.
  • The creator boasted that the game used zero texture or sound files, generating all assets, dialogue, and mechanics via code.
  • ZeniMax Media issued a cease-and-desist order on October 1, forcing the creator to privatize and take down the project.
  • The browser game was heavily criticized by the Fallout community for its janky mechanics, bizarre AI-generated ghouls, and lack of human artistry.
  • While this fan project is gone, official remasters of Fallout 3 and Fallout: New Vegas remain confirmed as in development.

Three Days of 'Post-War Slop'

On September 28, a social media user going by CHRIS FIRST revealed what they claimed was a playable Fallout game running directly in a web browser. Dubbed 'Fallout: New York,' the project was proudly advertised as being constructed entirely within Claude Opus 5.5. According to the creator, the game featured zero texture files and zero sound files, with every single building, weapon, dialogue tree, and face generated purely by code. While AI enthusiasts cheered the technical concept, actual Fallout fans were far less impressed, quickly pointing out the horrific visual jank and soulless asset generation.

The Banhammer Strikes Back

The project's survival was incredibly short-lived. By October 1, ZeniMax Media's legal team stepped in. CHRIS FIRST shared an image of the cease-and-desist letter, which outlined ZeniMax's legal obligation to enforce its trademarks against unauthorized uses that could dilute or damage the brand's reputation. The creator complained on social media, asking Bethesda to make a game so they 'don’t have to,' but the damage was done. The site was privatized, leaving behind a community that was more than happy to see it go.

Why New York Doesn't Work

Beyond the legal and ethical issues of using generative AI to hijack Bethesda's intellectual property, the project showed a fundamental misunderstanding of Fallout lore. In the established canon, New York is widely understood to be a smoldering, highly irradiated crater. There is a reason Bethesda has never set a major game there—there is simply nothing left to explore. Trying to force a bustling, AI-generated city into this specific setting felt incredibly out of touch for anyone who actually knows the universe.

Real Fallout Projects on the Horizon

If you are craving a return to the wasteland, you do not have to rely on machine-generated code. We already have official confirmation that remasters of Fallout 3 and Fallout: New Vegas are in the pipeline, alongside ongoing updates for Fallout Shelter. These projects are being handled by actual human developers who respect the lore and the art style of the franchise. While we do not have exact release dates for these remasters yet, they are a far better alternative to browser-based experiments.

Why fans are talking about this: Generative AI can mimic a Pip-Boy, but it can never replicate the human heart and world-building that makes Fallout worth playing.

ZeniMax's swift legal action shows they are actively protecting the integrity of the franchise as we look toward the future. While we wait for concrete release windows on the official Fallout 3 and New Vegas remasters, we can rest easy knowing the wasteland remains safe from low-effort AI clones.
