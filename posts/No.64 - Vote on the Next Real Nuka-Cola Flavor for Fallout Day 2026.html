Cast your vote for the new real-world Nuka-Cola Twist flavor as Bethesda and Jones Soda kickoff Fallout Day 2026 celebrations with in-person events.

Bethesda and Jones Soda are teaming up for Project T.W.I.S.T., letting fans choose the official flavor for a new Nuka-Cola Twist release.

Official plans for the Fallout Day Celebrations in 2026 are already taking shape, confirmed in a fresh community bulletin shared through the official Fallout 76 Steam news hub. Alongside a new teaser video that's making the rounds, Bethesda announced an interactive collaboration with Jones Soda titled Project T.W.I.S.T. Most notably, players will get an actual vote in deciding what a physical, real-world bottle of Nuka-Cola Twist will taste like before it rolls off the bottling line. It's a rare move that puts the flavor profile directly in the hands of the people who actually drink the stuff, marking a shift toward more community-driven merchandise as we look toward the franchise's future milestones.

Real-World Nuka-Cola Returns with Project T.W.I.S.T.

If your display shelves already hold empty Jones Soda bottles from earlier promotional runs, clear off another spot. Bethesda is teaming back up with the craft soda maker for Project T.W.I.S.T. In game lore—specifically introduced during Fallout 76's Nuka-World on Tour carnival update—Nuka-Cola Twist is known for its distinctive swirling label and its randomized, mystery-box buff effect. In the game, you never quite know what stats you're going to get when you pop the cap. Translating that concept into the real world could have gone in any direction, but instead of picking a flavor in a corporate test kitchen and shipping it out, Bethesda is putting the decision up for a fan vote. You'll actually have a say in whether Twist lands as something tart, sweet, citrusy, or entirely unexpected. It’s a fun way to bring a bit of that in-game RNG into the real world, minus the radiation.

In-Person Events on the Horizon

The announcement post didn't stop at soda; it also put in-person Fallout gatherings back on the official schedule. The Steam bulletin arrived with a dedicated video teaser and explicit mention of real-world events tied directly to the Fallout Day celebrations. While exact cities, convention halls, or pop-up locations haven't been locked into the calendar publicly yet, getting an early confirmation is big for community groups. We've seen how these events can turn into massive hubs for the community, and having a teaser video drop this early suggests Bethesda is looking to make 2026 a significant milestone for the brand. Whether that means developer-hosted panels, regional fan meetups, or full-scale community activations where folks show up in full power armor and Vault suits, having dates on the horizon gives players and cosplayers plenty of runway to plan trips and coordinate builds.

What Details Are Still Missing

Because this announcement serves as an early flag planted on the calendar, plenty of fine print remains to be filled in. Bethesda hasn't published the formal voting portal yet, nor have they listed the specific flavor candidates we'll be choosing between. Distribution channels are another open question. Past Jones Soda runs, like Nuka-Cola Quantum and Nuka-Cola Victory, saw a mix of direct online orders and select retail stockists, with stock routinely flying off shelves in hours. We're hoping they've learned from the 'Quantum' rush of years past where bottles were harder to find than a pristine perfectly preserved pie. International availability is always the biggest headache for our community members outside the United States, so we'll be watching closely to see if Jones Soda and Bethesda expand their shipping footprint for this round to include fans in Europe and beyond.

Why Interactive Soda Drops Work

Video game merch can easily slide into cheap plastic knick-knacks nobody asked for, but Jones Soda collabs have always carried genuine charm among Fallout collectors. Giving players the steering wheel on a flavor profile makes the final product feel like a shared project rather than a generic promotional item. It also fits the whimsical, slightly unhinged corporate satire of the Nuka-Cola Corporation itself. If the community collectively decides to vote for something completely wild—like a heavy-handed ginger or a floral blend—that's about as true to the spirit of the wasteland as it gets. It keeps the community engaged during the long stretches between major game releases, giving us something tangible to argue about in Discord besides which faction has the best armor.

Why fans are talking about this: Letting the community pick the flavor profile turns a standard merch drop into an actual collaborative event heading into Fallout Day 2026.
