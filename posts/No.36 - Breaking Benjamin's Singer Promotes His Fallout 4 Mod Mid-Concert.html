Breaking Benjamin frontman Benjamin Burnley is using live concerts to promote his Fallout 4 mod, True First Person, following online community drama.

Editorial note: This article is based on press reporting and has not been confirmed by the developer or publisher.

According to a new report, rock star Benjamin Burnley is using his band's stage time to settle a score with a rival Fallout modder.

I've seen some wild drama in the Fallout modding scene over the years, but this might legitimately take the crown. As reported by Rock Paper Shotgun, Breaking Benjamin frontman Benjamin Burnley recently paused a live concert to pitch his own Fallout 4 mod directly to the crowd. While Bethesda hasn't weighed in on this bizarre crossover, the multi-platinum singer is apparently on a personal mission to settle a community grudge using his tour dates as a promotional megaphone.

Key facts

  • Reported by Rock Paper Shotgun; not yet confirmed by the developer or publisher.
  • Breaking Benjamin singer Benjamin Burnley promoted his Fallout 4 mod, 'True First Person,' during a live gig in New Jersey.
  • Burnley claimed he is promoting the mod nightly to out-download a community member who bullied his work.
  • The mod is an F4SE plugin that keeps the player in first-person view, even during workshop mode.
  • The mod has just over 2,300 unique downloads on Nexus Mods and carries an 'AI-assisted' tag.

A Mid-Concert Pitch for 'True First Person'

According to the Rock Paper Shotgun report, concertgoers at a recent Breaking Benjamin gig in Holmdel, New Jersey, got a highly unusual four-minute interlude between songs. Instead of typical rock-show banter or city shoutouts, lead singer Benjamin Burnley took to the microphone to pitch his custom Fallout 4 mod. The moment was captured on video and shared online by a Reddit user going by im_all_ears. Burnley, who openly introduced himself as an amateur modder who tinkers with both Skyrim and Fallout 4, even had the venue crew project the title of his mod, 'True First Person,' up on the arena's massive display screen.

Settling a Modding Grudge on Stage

The motivation behind this arena-sized promotional push is deeply personal. As Burnley explained to the crowd, he felt pushed around by what he described as an online modding clique. Specifically, an unnamed community member allegedly told him his work was 'shit' and insisted he didn't know what he was doing. In response, Burnley told the audience he is announcing the mod on stage every single night to blow his detractor's numbers out of the water. Refusing to name his rival directly, Burnley bluntly asked the crowd to help him out-download the creator, declaring he would keep running the mid-show pitch nightly until the mod racks up one million downloads.

What the 'True First Person' Mod Actually Does

For those curious about what the software actually does under the hood, the report describes 'True First Person' as an F4SE (Fallout 4 Script Extender) DLL native plugin. The mod ensures players stay locked into an immersive first-person viewpoint throughout the game, allowing you to look down and see your character's body rather than floating as a detached camera. It also keeps you in first person while building in settlement workshop mode. Burnley even mentioned on stage that he planned to push an update for the mod live while on tour. To install and run it properly, you'll need the latest updated version of Fallout 4, alongside matching builds of the Script Extender and the Address Library for F4SE Plugins.

AI Assistance and Community Reception

Despite Burnley's nightly arena plugs, the mod still has a mountain to climb before reaching his million-download goal. The report notes that 'True First Person' has tallied just over 2,300 unique downloads since going live on Nexus Mods on September 1st. The listing also carries the platform's 'AI-assisted' tag, indicating the author used AI tools for limited code generation, texture upscaling, or concept work under developer supervision. Not everyone in the crowd was ready to talk load orders, either; roughly three minutes into the speech, an attendee could be heard shouting 'Play some music!' Burnley took it in stride, thanking the crowd for their patience before kicking back into the setlist.

Why fans are talking about this: If you ever needed proof that the Fallout modding scene is one of the most passionate—and occasionally petty—communities in gaming, a multi-platinum rock star launching a live-concert vendetta over Nexus downloads is it.

While we wait to see if Burnley's nightly plugs actually push 'True First Person' to the top of the Nexus charts, official confirmation or comment from Bethesda regarding this unusual situation remains pending. For now, it's just another wild chapter in our community's history. I'll be keeping an eye on the Nexus download counter to see if this rock-fueled rivalry actually moves the needle.
