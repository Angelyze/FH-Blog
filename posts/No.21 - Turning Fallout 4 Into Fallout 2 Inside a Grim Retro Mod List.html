A Fallout fan shared a mod list stripping the color from Fallout 4, transforming the Commonwealth into a dry, desolate, classic retro-style wasteland.

How a clever combination of weather, lighting, and texture mods strips away the Commonwealth's modern look for a classic, desolate vibe.

I love Fallout 4, but let's be honest: sometimes the Commonwealth feels a bit too vibrant, almost cozy, compared to the grim ruins of the classic Black Isle games. If you've ever wanted to swap the colorful, overgrown Boston skyline for something that feels like the harsh, sun-scorched dirt of the West Coast in 1998, you definitely aren't alone. Over on r/Fallout, community member /u/CollectionFuture3028 recently put together a clean, practical load order designed to do exactly that, stripping out the modern sheen to rebuild that vintage, desolate atmosphere.

Key facts

  • Shared by Reddit user /u/CollectionFuture3028 on the r/Fallout community.
  • Combines 12 specific mods to overhaul Fallout 4's visuals, weather, LODs, and enemy variety.
  • Features environmental mods like Dry Wasteland, Dilapidated Roads, Barren Trees, and Less Trees to remove foliage.
  • Uses Tweaked Fallout 3 Lightning and ENB Classic to dial in a harsh, desaturated retro aesthetic.
  • Mixes in More Feral Ghouls, A Zombie Mod, Defective Synths, and classic Advanced Power Armor.

Stripping the Green from Boston

The biggest hurdle in making the Commonwealth feel like the classic West Coast is the sheer amount of lingering life in the vanilla game. Fallout 4 gives you colorful clapboard houses, autumn leaves, and relatively smooth asphalt. To break that down into an unforgiving desert vibe, the list stacks several targeted landscape mods: "Dry Wasteland," "Dilapidated Roads," "Barren Trees," and "Less Trees." Together, they scrape away the undergrowth and kill off the canopies. Boston ends up looking thoroughly baked, cracked, and exposed—much closer to the desolate dirt tiles of the original isometric map where every trek between towns felt perilous.

Classic Lighting and Retro Shadows

Visual atmosphere isn't just about textures; it's about how the sun hits the dirt. The classic titles had a distinct, harsh contrast that modern game engines usually soften with volumetric godrays, ambient bloom, and warm color grading. This setup bypasses that by pairing "Tweaked Fallout 3 Lightning" with "ENB Classic." Pulling in that recognizable, slightly desaturated skybox and layering a muted ENB profile over the engine strips out the modern AAA gloss. The world instantly feels older, colder, and far more hostile to walk through alone.

Desolate Foes and Classic Armor

You can't sell a retro wasteland vibe without matching the threats you encounter along the road. The load order leans heavily into decaying, unpredictable enemies by combining "More Feral Ghouls" with "A Zombie Mod" and "Defective Synths." Instead of clean skirmishes against standard raiders, you get swarmed by shambling horrors and broken mechanical wanderers across open terrain. To complete the classic look, the author brought in "Advanced Power Armor," restoring the menacing silhouette of the Enclave from the late 90s so that late-game armored encounters hit with the exact weight they used to.

Keeping Performance Intact

Overhauling a game's entire landscape can easily tank your frame rate, especially when you start removing vanilla optimization systems. To keep the game playable, the list includes "FOLIP (LOD)" alongside "50 Shades of Rust." FOLIP helps manage Level of Detail rendering so your PC doesn't choke on the newly desolated horizons, while the rust textures keep the industrial areas looking appropriately decayed without requiring massive system resources. It's a smart, practical inclusion for anyone actually looking to play through a full campaign with this setup rather than just taking screenshots.

Why fans are talking about this: You don't need a full remake to capture the bleak, desolate spirit of the classic isometric games; sometimes, the right combination of weather, rust, and barren trees is all it takes to bring the West Coast desert to Boston.

It is always great to see how much life—or in this case, death—players can squeeze out of Fallout 4's engine. If you are tired of the same old green Commonwealth, this community-made load order is a fantastic way to experience a classic, desolate vibe. It is fan projects like this that keep the older games' spirits alive in the modern era.
