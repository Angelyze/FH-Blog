Bethesda has released a new update for Fallout 4 Creations that could break your active mods. Here is how to protect your save files and load order.

Bethesda Game Studios has released a new Creations update for Fallout 4, warning players to back up their load orders and save files immediately.

If you have spent hours meticulously crafting the perfect modded playthrough of Fallout 4, you might want to pause your game—or at least your auto-updates. Bethesda Game Studios has officially rolled out a new update for Fallout 4's Creations system aimed at delivering general improvements, as reported by Eurogamer. While updates are usually good news for modern titles, for those of us still wandering the Commonwealth a decade later, it comes with a heavy caveat: it is highly likely to disrupt your active mods. We've seen this movie before, and it usually ends with a broken load order and a lot of troubleshooting.

Key facts

  • Confirmed via official Bethesda communications; coverage from Eurogamer.
  • Bethesda released a new update for Fallout 4 Creations on August 18 to deliver 'general improvements.'
  • Players are strongly advised to back up their save files and manually save their mod load orders before updating.
  • The update changes the game's version number, which frequently causes third-party mods to stop functioning.
  • Bethesda confirmed a similar Creations update is currently in the works for Skyrim, with details coming soon.

Why This Update Is a Headache for Modders

For most modern games, a patch is cause for celebration. But for a decade-old title like Fallout 4, a surprise update often triggers a collective groan from the community. The issue isn't necessarily that Bethesda is changing core gameplay mechanics or fixing bugs we've lived with since 2015. Instead, even minor tweaks to the Creations system alter the game's version number. Many complex mods, especially those relying on script extenders or interlocking dependencies, are hardcoded to look for specific version numbers. When that number changes, the whole house of cards can come tumbling down, leading to instant crashes or broken save files. While Bethesda works on the next big thing, these mods are the lifeline sustaining the community, and every update feels like a bit of a gamble.

How to Protect Your Load Order

To mitigate the damage, Bethesda shared some preventative advice on X. Before you let your platform download the patch, log in and manually save your mod load order. Bethesda noted that once your load order is saved, you will be able to re-download your entire Creations list at once when the update is live and installed. Additionally, make sure to manually back up your physical save files. If a critical mod breaks and you load into a save without it, you risk permanently corrupting that character's progress. It is a tedious chore, but it beats losing a 100-hour survival run just because a script decided it didn't recognize the new build version.

The Abandoned Mod Dilemma

The real pain point for the community isn't the active mods; it's the abandoned ones. When a patch drops, active mod authors usually scramble to release compatibility updates. However, many of Fallout 4's most essential, game-changing mods were uploaded years ago by creators who have long since moved on from the scene. Because many players use a series of interlocking and interdependent mods, the failure of one foundational mod can cause a domino effect. If one of those older mods breaks under this new update, players are left stranded unless another community member steps up to write a patch or a workaround. It's the classic double-edged sword of a long-lived modding community.

Skyrim Players, You Are Next

If you're playing Skyrim instead of Fallout 4, don't get too comfortable. Bethesda also confirmed that a similar Creations update is actively in the works for the fantasy side of the house. While they didn't provide a concrete release date for the Skyrim patch, they promised to share more details 'soon.' Consider this your early warning system: start preparing your Skyrim load orders now so you aren't caught off guard when that patch inevitably drops. If you have a stable, 500-mod setup in Skyrim, you might want to set your Steam updates to 'only update when I launch the game' and use a third-party launcher to avoid the patch entirely.

Why fans are talking about this: A surprise update is a reminder that in the Bethesda ecosystem, stability is always temporary.

While Bethesda works behind the scenes on the next Elder Scrolls and Fallout 5, these legacy updates keep the old engines ticking, even if they cause some temporary friction. We don't have a release window for the upcoming Skyrim patch yet, but we'll be keeping a close eye on our Discord and community spaces for any workarounds as players test the new Fallout 4 build. It’s frustrating to see a stable game get shaken up, but it’s the price we pay for a game that Bethesda is still technically supporting a decade later. Hang in there, and don't forget to back up those saves before you hit update.
