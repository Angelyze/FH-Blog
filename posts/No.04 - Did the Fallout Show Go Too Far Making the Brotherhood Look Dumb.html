Fallout fans are debating if the TV show's depiction of the Brotherhood of Steel as "bumbling idiots" undermines the faction's game lore.

A viral community thread highlights why the Brotherhood's car-destroying antics in the TV show feel like a massive departure from the games.

If you watched the Fallout TV show, there’s a good chance one specific scene left you scratching your head. I'm talking about the moment the Brotherhood of Steel decides to blow up a pre-war car just for the hell of it, complete with some casual grenade juggling. Over on Reddit, fans are starting to ask if this goofy behavior is a sign of a deeper, unexamined decline—or just a fundamental misunderstanding of the faction. As someone who has spent way too many hours reading terminal entries about Brotherhood protocol across the games, seeing knights treat pre-war tech like target practice definitely caught my attention.

Key facts

  • A popular Reddit thread by u/Overwatch_Sniper criticizes the TV show's depiction of the Brotherhood of Steel.
  • The critique focuses on a scene where Brotherhood members casually destroy a pre-war car while juggling grenades.
  • Fans argue the classic Brotherhood would have sent scribes to examine technology before ever considering destroying it.
  • The community is calling for future seasons to properly contextualize the faction's apparent deterioration and internal civil war.

The Car Scene That Sparked the Debate

It’s hard to forget the image of Power Armor-clad knights acting less like elite techno-monks and more like bored teenagers with high explosives. In a thread over on r/Fallout titled "Wait, so we can all agree this was dumb, right?", user u/Overwatch_Sniper pointed out how jarring it was to watch the Brotherhood casually wreck a pre-war vehicle. In the games, technology is treated with near-religious reverence. Here, they're juggling grenades and blowing up salvage for kicks. It makes for a funny visual on TV, but for long-time lore nerds who remember how seriously the Brotherhood takes tech preservation, it felt completely out of character.

Where Did the Codex Go?

The core of the complaint lies in how much the Brotherhood's founding principles seem to have withered away. Historically, even the most aggressive or radical chapters of the Brotherhood adhered to a strict codex. If knights came across pre-war machinery, standard operating procedure was to have scribes document, analyze, and catalog it first. Blowing up salvage just for entertainment is the exact opposite of their founding mission. As the Reddit thread points out, without that structural discipline, the Brotherhood ends up looking less like a tragic, misguided military order and more like "bumbling idiots in Power Armor" juggling grenades in the desert.

The Need for Real Context

Showing a faction in decay isn't inherently bad writing, provided the show actually addresses why it's happening. The West Coast Brotherhood has been in decline for a long time in the timeline, and an internal civil war or cultural rot makes for a fantastic narrative hook. But right now, that deterioration hasn't really been fleshed out on screen. The thread highlights a very real concern among players: fans want the showrunners to contextualize this decline so it stays respectful to the faction's established characterization in the games, rather than just using knights as comic relief.

Can Season Two Save the Lore?

There is still plenty of room to fix this moving forward. With future seasons in development, the writers have a clear opportunity to dive deeper into the root causes of the Brotherhood's internal rift and crumbling standards. If the show frame this undisciplined, reckless behavior as a symptom of a dying chapter losing its core identity, the car scene actually makes a lot of sense retroactively. However, u/Overwatch_Sniper admitted to a lingering fear shared by many in the community: that the show might simply ignore the nuance and continue depicting the Brotherhood as a monolith of incompetent muscle.

Why fans are talking about this: Power Armor doesn't make you a Knight if you've forgotten why you put it on in the first place.

I love seeing the Fallout world expanded on screen, but the factions work best when their actions reflect the lore that built them. Showing the Brotherhood falling apart from within is a great story to tell—if the writers actually take the time to explain the drop in discipline. I'll definitely be watching closely next season to see if they address the cracks in the codex or keep leaning into the goofiness.
