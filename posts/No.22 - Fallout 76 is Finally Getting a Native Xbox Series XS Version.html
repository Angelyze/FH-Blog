Fallout 76 is getting a native Xbox Series X|S upgrade. Xbox Insiders can join the first-ever console PTS today to test 60 FPS and 4K support.

Bethesda has opened the first-ever Xbox Public Test Server to let console players test targeted 60 FPS, 4K resolution, and native Series X|S performance.

Xbox players have spent years watching PC players get all the Public Test Server fun, but that finally changes today. Bethesda has officially announced the first-ever Fallout 76 Public Test Server for Xbox Series X|S, as first reported on Xbox Wire. This isn't just a minor patch or another 'FPS Boost' tweak; it's a major step toward a native current-gen console version of the game. If you've spent any time wandering the Mire or trying to build a complex C.A.M.P. on console, you know that the hardware has been capable of more than the game was giving it. This move is all about making the game feel 'more comfortable' to play, according to Bethesda, and it’s a milestone we’ve been waiting for since the Series X launched.

Key facts

  • Confirmed via official Bethesda communications; coverage from Xbox Wire.
  • Xbox Insiders with Fallout 76 or Game Pass can join the PTS starting today.
  • The test features targeted 60 FPS, 4K resolution (Series X), and VRR support.
  • Progression does not transfer to the live game, and the Atomic Shop is disabled.
  • Feedback is being collected directly via the official Bethesda Studios Discord.

What's Actually on the Test Server

For this first console PTS run, Bethesda is focusing heavily on performance rather than new story content. If you jump in, you will be testing a native Xbox Series X|S build designed to use the console hardware far more efficiently than the backward-compatible version we've been using. The main targets are a smooth 60 FPS on both Series X and S, 4K resolution on the Series X, and Variable Refresh Rate (VRR) support for those with compatible displays. Bethesda mentioned that this native update allows them to utilize the hardware more efficiently today and in the future, which suggests that future updates might finally stop being held back by the limitations of the older Xbox One architecture. It is all about making the game feel tighter, more responsive, and visually cleaner on your controller.

How the Character Snapshot Works

You do not have to start from scratch to help out with the testing. Bethesda takes a snapshot of your current live characters—including your weapons, armor, and C.A.M.P. builds—so you can play with your actual gear. Just keep in mind that any progress, levels, or rare loot you earn in the PTS will not transfer back to the live game. Also, the Atomic Shop and seasonal content are turned off here to keep the focus on the technical performance. If you need supplies to test things out, look for the PTS Locker right outside Vault 76. These lockers usually provide easy access to mods, ammo, and weapons so you can stress-test the performance in heavy combat without burning through your actual live-game resources. Your PTS character progression also doesn't persist between different test cycles, as they take a fresh snapshot before every major PTS phase.

How to Get In Right Now

Getting in is straightforward if you are already in the Xbox ecosystem, but it does require a separate download. Boot up your console, open the Xbox Insider Hub app (download it from the store if you don't have it), and head to the 'Previews' section. Look for 'Fallout 76 (Public Test Server),' hit join, and wait for the registration to process. Once it is done, you will be redirected to the Xbox Store to download the separate PTS client. Be prepared for a large file size, as this is essentially a full second version of the game. If the store gives you an error when you try to download it, Bethesda recommends waiting about 15 minutes before trying again to let the registration sync up. If you don't see the preview at all, double-check that you actually own the game or have an active Game Pass subscription on that account.

The Catch: Bugs and English Only

Because this is a true test environment, expect some rough edges. You are going to run into bugs, crashes, and unfinished features—that is literally the point of a PTS. You're helping them find the breaks before the update goes out to everyone else. Additionally, because this content is still in development, most of the new additions have not been translated yet, meaning the entire PTS experience is strictly limited to English for now. If you find a bug—and you likely will—Bethesda wants you to head to their official Discord. You'll need to visit the #server-roles channel to grab the PTS role, which unlocks the dedicated feedback and bug reporting channels. It's a bit of a process, but it's the most direct way to make sure the developers actually see your performance data.

Why fans are talking about this: A native current-gen console version is exactly what Fallout 76 needs to keep running smoothly for another five years.

While Bethesda hasn't given us a concrete release date for when these native Xbox Series X|S upgrades will hit the live servers, this PTS is a massive milestone for console players. It’s the first time we’ve been invited into the kitchen to see how the game is being cooked for the latest hardware. We will be keeping a close eye on how the performance holds up during the test, especially in high-intensity areas like Scorched Earth or during busy seasonal events. If you've been waiting for a reason to jump back into Appalachia, a native 60 FPS boost might just be the smoothest way to do it.
