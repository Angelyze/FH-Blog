The Las Vegas Atomic Museum expanded its World of Fallout exhibit with Season 2 costumes, an interactive Overseer’s desk, and a reactive Mister Handy.

Bethesda and the National Atomic Testing Museum have added two new galleries packed with show props and real-world nuclear history.

It has been exactly one year since the 'World of Fallout' exhibit first opened its doors at the National Atomic Testing Museum in Las Vegas, and they aren't slowing down for the anniversary. Xbox Wire just confirmed that the exhibit has officially doubled its footprint. If you’re heading to 'Old Vegas' anytime soon, you’ll find two new galleries packed with props from the Prime Video series and interactive gear. It’s a rare, slightly surreal chance to see how the game’s dark satire of the Atomic Age stacks up against actual Cold War artifacts and the genuine paranoia of the era.

Key facts

  • Confirmed via official Bethesda communications; coverage from Xbox Wire.
  • The exhibit now features two new galleries including an interactive Overseer’s Desk.
  • New additions include on-camera costumes from Season 2 of the Fallout TV show.
  • A full-scale Mister Handy replica by Dragonfoot3D reacts if you get too close.
  • Visitors can hack terminals to read real-life anecdotes from the Nevada Test Site.

Bringing the Mojave to Life

The collaboration with the University of Nevada, Las Vegas (UNLV) is a standout part of this expansion. Students and staff from the Entertainment, Engineering, and Design program built the replicas of the New Vegas and Lucky 38 signs, complete with that classic neon glow. It wasn't just a commission; it was a hands-on learning project that brought the landmarks we’ve spent hundreds of hours wandering around in-game into the physical world. Seeing these signs sit alongside real-world 1950s posters and pulp books is eye-opening. It turns out the '50s aesthetic Fallout parodies—the bright colors masking deep nuclear anxiety—wasn't actually that much of an exaggeration.

Hacking the Overseer’s Terminal

The new Overseer’s Desk isn't just a static prop for photos. It features functional terminals where you can try your hand at hacking, just like in the games. If you manage to get in, you’ll find spooky, real-life anecdotes from the Nevada Test Site’s history. But the interaction doesn't stop at the screen; the museum encourages you to actually scavenge the environment. Keep an eye out for supply caches and hidden historical artifacts tucked away in drawers and cabinets throughout the room. And even if you're a pro at the hacking minigame and never break a pick, fail it once on purpose—the custom warning message that pops up is a nice nod for the fans.

Don’t Touch the Mister Handy

You’ll be greeted by a life-size Mister Handy replica created by fan fabricator Dragonfoot3D—the same person responsible for the museum's Deathclaw and terminal replicas. While he looks great, he’s strictly look-but-don't-touch. His eyes are programmed to react threateningly if you get too close, which is a fair warning for anyone who remembers their first encounter with a hostile robot in the wasteland. While you're there, you can also take part in 'Fallout Finds,' a museum-wide scavenger hunt. It uses QR codes to link historical artifacts in the main museum to their in-game counterparts, making the whole building feel like a giant lore entry.

Season 2 Costumes and Easter Eggs

For those tracking the Prime Video series, the expansion includes actual on-camera costumes from the upcoming Season 2. There are also some deep-cut easter eggs for history buffs, like an autographed photo of Edward Teller—the father of the hydrogen bomb—which was originally given to museum founder Troy Wade. You might even spot a photo of the Bethesda staff who helped put the exhibit together tucked away in the Vault. There’s even a nod to Perry Como; while his music hasn't hit the games yet, it was featured in the Season 1 intro and Season 2 finale. Since he was born in Canonsburg, PA—the real-world inspiration for Vault 33—the connection is a clever bit of world-building that bridges the show and reality.

Why fans are talking about this: Seeing the line between Fallout’s satire and real nuclear history blur is exactly why this museum works.

The expansion is open now at the National Atomic Testing Museum, marking one year since the exhibit first launched in November 2025. While we're still waiting on firm dates for more Season 2 news, these physical displays are the best way to bridge the gap between the screen and the real world. If you find yourself in Vegas, it's worth the detour away from the slots to see the history that made the games possible.
