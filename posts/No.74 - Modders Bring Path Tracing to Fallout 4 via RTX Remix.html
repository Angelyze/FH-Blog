Modders have reportedly bypassed RTX Remix limits to bring path tracing to Fallout 4 and Dark Souls, according to a new report from Wccftech.

Editorial note: This article is based on press reporting and has not been confirmed by the developer or publisher.

A new report indicates modders are bypassing pipeline hurdles to bring full path tracing to Fallout 4 using NVIDIA's Remix tool.

I’ve been watching the RTX Remix scene pretty closely since NVIDIA launched the open beta back in January 2024, but the consensus was always that Fallout 4 was out of reach. The platform was strictly built for older games running fixed-function graphics pipelines—the kind of tech that powered Morrowind, not the more modern Creation Engine. But according to a new report from Wccftech, modders have managed to push past those engine limitations to bring path tracing to newer shader-heavy titles, including our beloved Commonwealth. While neither Bethesda nor NVIDIA has officially endorsed these projects, the community progress reveals a major breakthrough for those of us who have been waiting for a true visual overhaul that goes beyond just higher-resolution textures.

Key facts

  • Reported by Wccftech; not yet confirmed by the developer or publisher.
  • NVIDIA RTX Remix launched in Open Beta in January 2024, originally targeting pre-2005 games with fixed-function pipelines.
  • Post-2005 games like Fallout 4 use programmable runtime shaders that typically block RTX Remix draw call interception.
  • Modders have reportedly bypassed these shader barriers to inject path tracing into both Fallout 4 and Dark Souls.
  • The breakthrough allows for real-time path-traced global illumination and ray-traced lighting in the Creation Engine.

Breaking Out of the Fixed-Function Pipeline

As detailed by Wccftech, NVIDIA originally designed RTX Remix around older titles that send standardized geometry and texture data. Because older fixed-function games lacked complex runtime code, Remix could easily step in, intercept draw calls, and inject path tracing, custom PBR assets, and DLSS. It was a dream for games from the early 2000s, but anything released after 2005—like Fallout 4—shifted heavily toward per-pixel programmable shaders. Those opaque shaders normally act like a brick wall, preventing Remix from capturing scene data cleanly. This is exactly why titles in the 'modern' era of the Bethesda catalog were previously considered off-limits for the tool, leaving us stuck with traditional screen-space reflections and baked lighting.

Path Tracing Comes to the Commonwealth

Despite those technical barriers, modders have reportedly cracked the workflow needed to make RTX Remix function alongside modern programmable shaders. According to the report, community developers worked out solutions to bypass the runtime code restrictions that usually stop draw call interception dead in its tracks. For Fallout 4, this is a massive deal. It opens the door to real-time path-traced global illumination, which fundamentally changes how light behaves in the world. We're talking about light that actually bounces off surfaces and carries color with it, rather than the 'good enough' hacks we’ve been using for the last decade. It’s the kind of upgrade that usually requires a developer to overhaul the entire engine, but the community is doing it from the outside in.

Why This Technical Leap Matters for Players

Anyone who has spent hours tweaking ENB setups or wrestling with custom shadow distance settings knows how easily Fallout 4 can stretch performance and still look a bit flat in the wrong light. Replacing standard shadow maps with path tracing changes everything about the atmosphere. Think about how light bounces off damp concrete in the shadows of Downtown Boston, how it filters through the thick green haze of a Radstorm, or how it reflects inside the sterile, tight corridors of a Vault. It isn't just about 'better' graphics; it's about a consistent physical reality for the lighting that the 2015 version of the Creation Engine just wasn't engineered to handle. It gives an unexpected second life to a game that many felt had already hit its ceiling with the recent Next-Gen update.

Early Stage Progress and Missing Details

It is smart to keep your performance expectations grounded for now. Wccftech notes that while getting path tracing active in these shader-based titles is a huge milestone, detailed performance metrics and stability reports are still very limited. We also saw similar progress mentioned for Dark Souls, suggesting this isn't just a one-off fluke for Fallout. However, there is currently no public release date or a step-by-step setup guide for the Fallout 4 RTX Remix mod. Running path tracing on top of Bethesda's notoriously dense urban areas—which already struggle with draw calls—will undoubtedly demand some serious hardware. You’re likely going to need a high-end 40-series card to even get this into a playable state once it eventually goes public.
