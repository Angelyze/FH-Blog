A brilliant fan concept pitches Michigan as the ultimate setting for Fallout 5, complete with driveable cars, Faygo, and a Battle Creek Enclave.

A Michigan native's detailed pitch shows how Motor City, the Midwestern Brotherhood, and mutant squirrels could redefine the wasteland.

I’ve spent way too much time scrolling through r/Fallout lately, but a recent pitch for a Michigan-based setting actually stopped me in my tracks. It’s not just another 'wouldn't it be cool if my hometown was in the game' post. A Michigan native laid out a roadmap that feels like a genuine design document. With Bethesda finally giving us drivable land vehicles in Starfield, the 'Motor City' setting isn't just a thematic fit—it’s a technical necessity for the next engine. This concept uses real local history, weird regional quirks, and actual lore from the TV show map to build a wasteland I’d genuinely want to explore. It's grounded, it's gritty, and it understands exactly why we play these games: to see how the world breaks in specific, localized ways.

Key facts

  • Pitches Michigan as a vehicle-focused map utilizing Bethesda's updated engine capabilities for a 'state-sized' experience.
  • Features three main factions: the Battle Creek Enclave (Fort Custer), the Grand Rapids-based Midwestern Brotherhood, and a self-sufficient Michigan Militia.
  • Utilizes the Vault map from Fallout: The Show to place a crucial reactor-powered Vault in the Grand Rapids area.
  • Includes a post-game 'Toledo War' historical reference and a high-level endurance check based on the massive meals at Tonie's on I-75.
  • Centers the main conflict around the intact manufacturing plants of Detroit for power armor and vehicle production.

The Case for Motor City Wheels

The core hook of this concept relies on modern tech. Since Starfield introduced drivable land vehicles, the next Fallout engine will almost certainly have to support cars. If you're going to build a massive, state-sized map around driving, Michigan is the only logical choice. The pitch sets up a wasteland where vehicles aren't just a gimmick, but the literal engine of the regional economy and conflict. We’re talking about a map that stretches across the lower peninsula, where the distance between cities actually matters because you have the horsepower to bridge it. It shifts the gameplay loop from 'walking between ruins' to 'maintaining your rig' to survive the stretches of highway between Grand Rapids and Detroit.

Three Factions Colliding in the Ruins

Instead of the usual good-versus-evil setup, the pitch establishes a three-way power struggle with real stakes. The Battle Creek Enclave operates out of Fort Custer and a nearby Air National Guard base. They’re doing the usual villainous shadow-government stuff, but the author suggests making them a viable joinable faction for a player-led victory. Meanwhile, the Midwestern Brotherhood is holed up in Grand Rapids. They’ve absorbed local Vault dwellers (citing the Vault map seen in the Fallout TV show) and are using a Vault reactor to power the entire city. Finally, the Michigan Militia rules the north—a highly independent group of 'gun-obsessed nutjobs' who treat weapon maintenance as a point of honor. Mercifully, the author notes these guys wouldn't be 'another Minutemen'—they actually survive on their own without the player having to save every single settlement.

The Road to Detroit and the Upper Peninsula

The campaign map is built around natural choke points and real geography. Lansing is reimagined as a highly radioactive, freshly nuked no-go zone that forces players to find creative detours. To reach the late-game content in the Upper Peninsula, you'll have to fight a massive battle to secure the Mackinac Bridge, which acts as a literal gate to the northern half of the map. Everything culminates in a struggle for Detroit's pristine pre-war factories. Each faction has a different vision for the assembly lines: the Brotherhood wants Power Armor and ammo, the Enclave wants to build planes, and the Militia just wants to get the car industry back on its feet. The 'Battle for the Arbor' (Ann Arbor) serves as the final staging point before the push into the city ruins.

Juggalos, Faygo, and Giant Squirrels

What makes this pitch great is the hyper-local humor that feels right at home in Fallout. The author suggests replacing Nuka-Cola with a regional soda brand reminiscent of Faygo, complete with 10 to 15 bizarre flavors to collect. Ann Arbor is reimagined as a hippie haven overrun by genuinely massive mutant squirrels—a nod to the city's real-life squirrel population. In a classic Fallout twist, 'evil scientists' from the University of Michigan are experimenting on the locals, but the hippies are so laid back they haven't even noticed. They’ve even accidentally domesticated a Deathclaw by getting it high. There's also a nod to Frankenmuth, suggested by Reddit user persvill, featuring German-themed ghouls and a cult centered around Bronner’s, the massive year-round Christmas store. They even included a 'Tonie's on I-75' reference where you’d have to pass an Endurance check just to finish one of their massive post-nuke meals.

Why fans are talking about this: A great Fallout setting needs more than just ruined landmarks; it needs local soul, and this Michigan concept has it in spades—from the 'Toledo War' post-game content to the Great Milenko references.

It is easy to write off fan concepts as pure wish-listing, but this one works because it respects the geography and the lore we already have. It takes the game beyond the typical East Coast ruins while keeping that classic, slightly unhinged dark humor intact. Whether it's the 'gun honor' of the northern militia or the sheer terror of a radioactive Mackinac Bridge crossing, this is the kind of world-building that makes the franchise feel alive. If Bethesda is looking for inspiration for the next decade, they could do a lot worse than heading to Motor City.
