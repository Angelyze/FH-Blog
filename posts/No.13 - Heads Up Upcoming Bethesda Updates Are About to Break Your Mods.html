Bethesda is warning Fallout 4 and Skyrim players to back up their saves ahead of upcoming updates that are highly likely to break third-party mods.

Editorial note: This article is based on press reporting and has not been confirmed by the developer or publisher.

According to a new report, Bethesda is rolling out stability patches that could disrupt your carefully curated load orders as early as Tuesday.

If you’ve spent the last few months finally getting your Fallout 4 load order to stop crashing after the chaos of the 'next-gen' update, I have some news you aren’t going to like. It is time to pause your automatic updates again. A new report from Polygon highlights a fresh warning directly from Bethesda, advising players to back up their saves and load orders ahead of an impending patch. While we are still waiting on the granular patch notes to see what is actually being changed under the hood, the looming update already has the PC community bracing for impact. If you value your stability, you'll want to pay attention to the timing on this one.

Key facts

  • Reported by Polygon; not yet confirmed by the developer or publisher.
  • Reported by Polygon via Bethesda social media alerts; the update targets PC players on Steam specifically.
  • Bethesda's official advice: 'Log into your game save if you haven’t done so in a while or manually save your load order.'
  • Fallout 4 is scheduled to receive its update first, on Tuesday, August 18, targeting 'general improvements' to the Creations system.
  • An update for Skyrim is also confirmed to follow shortly after, though a specific day hasn't been pinned down yet.
  • The primary concern is the breaking of script extenders and third-party mods that rely on specific executable versions.

The Dreaded Update Warning

Here we go again. According to the Polygon report, Bethesda recently posted a social media warning urging players of both Fallout 4 and Skyrim to log in and manually back up their game saves. The developer specifically stated: 'As a precaution, prior to these updates we encourage players to log into your game save if you haven’t done so in a while or manually save your load order.' The idea here is that once your load order is saved to their servers, you should theoretically be able to re-download your entire Creations list in one go once the patches are live. Fallout 4 is scheduled to get the first hit on Tuesday, August 18, with a focus on 'general improvements' to the internal Creations system. It sounds minor, but in the world of Bethesda modding, there is no such thing as a 'minor' update when it touches the executable file.

Why This is a Nightmare for Modders

For most modern games, a quick stability or system update is a welcome tune-up that you don't even think twice about. For Bethesda titles, it is usually a recipe for broken load orders and 'black screen' launches. The report notes that while some dedicated mod authors will jump to fix their work immediately after the patch drops, many classic mods are effectively 'orphaned.' If a mod creator has moved on from the scene, their work might be broken forever by this update. Mod creators are already bracing for an onslaught of unhappy fans asking why their games no longer work. If you rely on complex third-party mods to keep your Commonwealth looking decent, Tuesday is likely going to be a very frustrating day for your plugin list.

The Skyrim Connection

Fallout 4 isn't the only game in the crosshairs this time around. The report mentions that Skyrim is also slated for a similar update in the near future, though Bethesda hasn't given us a firm date for that one yet. This comes at a particularly busy time for the Skyrim community. Polygon points out that tens of thousands of players—over 34,000 on a random Monday—still log in daily on Steam. Many of these players recently adopted a revolutionary new graphics mod that significantly improves the game's visuals without tanking PC performance. An unexpected patch could put a sudden, screeching halt to those adventures, forcing players to choose between the 'general improvements' from Bethesda or the actual visual upgrades provided by the community.

How to Protect Your Game Right Now

Until we get the full patch notes and see the actual damage, the community is already moving into defensive positions. The report highlights that players on social media are actively advising each other to disable automatic updates on Steam immediately. To do this, right-click the game in your library, go to Properties, then Updates, and set it to 'Only update this game when I launch it.' If you only launch the game through a script extender like F4SE or SKSE, Steam won't trigger the update, allowing you to stay on the current, stable version of the game. It is a classic Bethesda community maneuver that we've had to perform dozens of times over the years, but it remains the only reliable way to keep a heavily modded save file from melting down.

Why fans are talking about this: When a developer updates a decade-old game to 'improve' things, it's usually the players and modders who end up doing the heavy lifting to fix the collateral damage.

While we wait for official confirmation on what these 'general improvements' actually entail, keeping your Steam updates paused is the only logical move. Stay safe out there, and seriously—back up those saves before Tuesday rolls around.
