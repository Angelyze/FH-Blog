Breaking Benjamin frontman Benjamin Burnley explains how a broken snare drum led to his viral mid-concert promo for his Fallout 4 first-person mod.

Editorial note: This article is based on press reporting and has not been confirmed by the developer or publisher.

A new report sheds light on why rock star Benjamin Burnley paused a concert to defend his custom Fallout 4 mod from online critics.

We've all seen some weird things happen at rock shows, but a frontman pausing mid-set to defend his Fallout 4 camera mod against Nexus Mods comment section drama is definitely up there. According to a report by Rock Paper Shotgun covering an interview with Nexus Mods, Breaking Benjamin singer Benjamin Burnley claims his viral mid-gig speech was mostly an ad-libbed effort to fill awkward dead air. While Bethesda hasn't weighed in on the rock star's modding hobby, Burnley explained that a busted snare drum, internet negativity, and a deep love for VR led directly to the creation of his 'True First Person' project.

Key facts

  • Reported by Rock Paper Shotgun; not yet confirmed by the developer or publisher.
  • Reported by Rock Paper Shotgun via an interview with Nexus Mods; not an official developer statement.
  • Singer Benjamin Burnley (modding under the handle 'Killemloud') claims a broken snare drum forced him to ad-lib on stage while road techs made repairs.
  • The mod in question, 'True First Person' for Fallout 4, was built to recreate his preferred VR camera experience while traveling on a tour bus.
  • Burnley's Bethesda modding roots trace back to using VorpX to play Fallout 3 and New Vegas in VR, alongside porting popular Skyrim mods.

The Snare Drum Incident

According to the Rock Paper Shotgun report, Burnley's mid-concert speech wasn't some calculated promotional stunt. Instead, his drummer busted a snare drum head mid-show, leaving the band standing around on stage in dead silence. Burnley, who releases his work on Nexus Mods under the handle Killemloud, had just published the first version of his Fallout 4 mod and immediately caught a wave of harsh feedback. As any longtime mod user knows, launch versions rarely reflect the final product because setups and expectations vary wildly. Standing around while stage techs rushed to swap the drumhead, Burnley felt every second drag out like an hour, so he used humor to vent about the online backlash and kill the silence.

A Contradiction in the Crowd

While Burnley framed the speech as a purely spontaneous ad-lib, Rock Paper Shotgun noted that a few details don't entirely line up. In the footage posted by an attendee, a fan in the crowd can clearly be heard shouting 'Play some music!' right in the middle of his speech, proving that not everyone in the audience was thrilled to hear about Nexus drama. The fan who uploaded the original clip did later add a note confirming that stage crew could be seen testing the kit during maintenance, but Burnley also admitted during his speech that he had been shouting out the mod 'every fucking night' on tour. That makes the 'one-off spur of the moment' explanation feel a bit stretched, even if the broken drum gave him a wider window to talk.

Why 'True First Person' Exists

The singer's real motivation for building the mod comes down to his VR habit. Burnley explained that he loves playing Skyrim VR and Fallout 4 VR, but hauling and calibrating a full virtual reality rig every single day inside a cramped tour bus is basically a non-starter. Whenever tour life forces him to play standard flat Fallout 4 on a laptop or monitor, he hates having the camera yank him out of first-person perspective during interactions. In VR, you never pull back to see your character's third-person body, so he built 'True First Person' specifically to recreate that seamless, locked-in immersion within the flat version of the game.

From VR Ports to Custom Mods

Burnley didn't just pick up the Creation Kit yesterday. His history with Bethesda games runs deep, starting back before official VR editions even existed. He initially relied on VorpX, an injection program used to force flat titles like Fallout 3 and Fallout: New Vegas into virtual reality headsets. In the interview, Burnley emphasized that mods are crucial for VR because they address fundamental mechanics rather than simple visuals—like actually being able to reach out and grab items in the game world. Before publishing standalone work like True First Person, he cut his teeth porting well-known Skyrim mods to VR, including Convenient Horses and Better Vampires, making his jump to Fallout 4 scripting a natural progression.

Why fans are talking about this: Sometimes the best mods aren't born out of high-concept design docs, but out of a traveling musician desperately trying to replicate his home VR setup on a tour bus.

It's always fun seeing high-profile musicians get just as hopelessly lost in Bethesda modding rabbit holes as the rest of us. Burnley may have bewildered a few rock fans who just wanted to hear their favorite tracks, but anyone who has spent hours troubleshooting load orders can relate to the impulse. Whether Bethesda ever acknowledges the Killemloud handle or not, he's clearly putting in real work behind the scenes.
